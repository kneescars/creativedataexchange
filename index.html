About Creative Data Exchange

New technology has been disrupting creative practice long before the invention of the paintbrush. Yet it has been some time since a single wave of innovation held the potential to fundamentally transform how we create — across artistic disciplines and at every stage of the creative process.

And as with many moments of profound change, the Data & AI hype emerged around serious ethical and safety concerns, as well as mysticism, fueling curiosity but also provoking unease, scepticism, and hesitation to engage.

"Creative Data Exchange" is the antidote to this. It provides a space for open, grounded conversations about working creatively with data — where you can share questions and ambitions, and find routes to answers in good company.

Creative Data Exchange is presented by the Contemporary Narratives Lab in partnership with Goldsmiths Futures of Creativity Institute (FOCI) and Fuel. It is supported by the Software Sustainability Institute via an SSI Fellowship 2026.

What are we inviting you for?

Up to 2 hours of an online conversation about creative practice, AI and data.

Beyond the Call

What happens after the conversation?

We are forming a community of interested artists, creatives, and researchers to keep the conversation going. You will be invited to join the Alan Turing Institutes AI & Arts group, where we can continue to meet online via the groups Slack space. We will have a Creative Data Exchange channel to be in touch with everyone who engages with the programme, and you also will have access to an international community of over 500 members who are arts practitioners, researchers, policymakers and funders, based in all continents, who are interested in working in the intersection of art and data.

Your contributions to the online calls will feed a free programme of in-person, hands-on, workshops to be advertised to the community first.

Who are we inviting? Experienced creative practitioners with little to no experience using data who are curious to learn more.

The Mission

Why are they facilitating these conversations?

We are passionate about art and technology but we have seen the recent advances increasing the digital divide. Those who had experience and felt confident to onboard the AI boat have done so. But there hasnt been much opportunity for artists who are unfamiliar with this technology to get clarity beyond the hype.

1

It is bad for technology development

Which gets narrower, involving a smaller set of artistic questions and practice.

2

It is bad for artists

Who may miss out on opportunities and debates that are crucial for their development and that of the creative industries more generally.

3

We want community-led change

To understand from this community what it takes to break those barriers and create opportunities for the community itself to drive this change.

4

Systemic implications

This debate has many implications for creative practice, not least related to copyrights, visibility and funding of their own work, digital or not.
